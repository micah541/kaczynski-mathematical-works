Errata and divergences · Lean 4 formalization

The mathematical works of Theodore J. Kaczynski

We formalized all eight sources, his published papers and his dissertation, in Lean 4 with Mathlib. Doing so required a blueprint that proves every result in full. This sheet lists each place where what we proved departs from the published text. Some are errors that needed a correction. Some are steps the sources cite without proof, which we proved or routed around. Some are arguments we replaced with different ones. The rest are items we did not formalize.

Corrections

Three statements in the sources are wrong as written, and one cited theorem needs a restriction on n. None of these affects a main theorem. Each one has a local fix.

Complementary intervals give non-closed sets

Error
Dissertation, Lemma 10 (every Fσ set is the union of a special family)
Source
At each stage, the new sets are formed as I ∩ Es+1, where I runs over the open complementary intervals of the earlier closed sets. These sets are claimed to be closed.
Problem
The intersection of a closed set with an open interval need not be closed. Take E0 = [−1, 0] and E1 = {0} ∪ {1/k : k ≥ 1}, which split at 0. The complementary interval (0, ∞) of E0 gives {1/k : k ≥ 1}, which is not closed.
Fix
Use the closed interval: Ī ∩ Es+1. With this change the lemma is proved. We also replaced the source’s subsequence argument for “Es+1 meets only finitely many complementary intervals” with a shorter argument that counts classes.

Blueprint lem:th-lemma10 · Lean TJK.Variant.exists_thesisSpecial_of_isFσ

Interior of a trapezoid

Error
Dissertation, definition of trapezoids before Lemmas 23–24 and Theorem 9
Source
A trapezoid is any set lying between a closed trapezoid T and its interior. Its area is claimed to be half the sum of the lengths of its traces on the lines y = 0 and y = 1.
Problem
The interior is taken in the plane, so it misses the two horizontal edges, and the area formula fails for sets that omit them. With infinite parameters, the degenerate sides ai = bi = ±∞ also break the formula.
Fix
Take the interior relative to the strip 0 ≤ y ≤ 1, and exclude sides with ai = bi = ±∞. Theorem 9 is unaffected: its proof only uses closed trapezoids with finite parameters.

Blueprint def:trapezoid · Lean TJK.Discont.closedTrapezoid, volume_closedTrapezoid

A constant in the modulus d(t)

Error
Trans. AMS 141, proof of (D) (realizing an Fσδ set with a prescribed boundary function)
Source
The proof defines d(t) from the moduli of continuity d1, d2 with a factor ½.
Problem
d1 and d2 are defined as suprema. With the factor ½, the estimate on |p − q| reaches the supremum itself. The definition of the supremum then gives nothing, because a strict inequality is needed.
Fix
Replace ½ by ¼: d(t) = min{¼ D(t/2), t/4}. The rest of the argument is unchanged.

Blueprint thm:construct-D · Lean TJK.Construct.exists_f_Q

Sutcliffe’s Theorem 2 needs n ≥ 4

Error
Math. Mag. 41, as cited in the proofs of the Theorem and Corollary
Source
The note cites Sutcliffe’s Theorem 2: a 2-digit solution exists for base n exactly when n + 1 is composite.
Problem
The direction “n + 1 composite ⇒ a 2-digit solution exists” fails for n = 3: 4 is composite, but base 3 has no 2-digit solution.
Fix
Kaczynski’s own results are not affected. He handles n = 2, 3 separately (his Lemma 2), and his Corollary is stated for n > 3. We prove both directions of Sutcliffe’s Theorem 2 for n ≥ 4, with explicit solutions (a, c, k) = (e − 1, n − e, d − 1) when n + 1 = de.

Blueprint prop:sut-two-composite, prop:sut-explicit · Lean TJK.Sutcliffe

Steps that rely on outside theorems

The sources quote these results without proof. In each case we either proved the result or replaced the argument that uses it.

The Jordan curve theorem, with accessibility

Cited
Trans. AMS 137, Lemma (used for the bounded harmonic counterexample)
Source
The Lemma covers general Jordan domains along disjoint arcs of the circle. It glues their boundaries into one Jordan curve and invokes the Jordan curve theorem, including accessibility of every boundary point.
What we did
For the domains actually built in the paper, we construct the accessing arc directly: s ↦ (1 − s) eiθ(s), bending around each domain that blocks the radius. This uses only the separation property IV of the paper’s intervals. The Jordan curve theorem is not used, and the Lemma in its general form is not formalized.

Blueprint lem:ha-access · Lean TJK.Harmonic.Intervals.accessible

Kierst’s theorem and normal functions

Cited
Proc. AMS 23, Lemma (a continuous function on the half-plane with a non-Borel set of asymptotic values)
Source
The proof combines Kierst’s theorem on asymptotic values of holomorphic functions with the Lehto–Virtanen and Bagemihl–Seidel results on normal functions. The paper remarks that an elementary proof exists, but omits it as a “messy construction”.
What we did
We give a new elementary proof that uses only the existence of an analytic non-Borel set. It builds the function on a binary tree of segments in the half-plane. This proof was reviewed independently. Kaczynski’s original argument appears as a remark and is not formalized.

Bagemihl’s ambiguous point theorem

Cited
Michigan Math. J. 13, remark after Corollary 4 (Gδ targets)
Source
The remark uses Bagemihl’s theorem (1955): for an arbitrary function on the disk, only countably many boundary points have two arcs with different limits.
What we did
The function in the remark is continuous, and that case has a short proof. Two disjoint connected open sets in the disk share at most three accessible boundary points, by the crosscut theorem. Classifying the ambiguous points by countably many such pairs gives countability. The general theorem, for arbitrary functions, is not needed and is not formalized.

Blueprint lem:two-domains, fact:bagemihl · Lean TJK.accessible_two_domains, TJK.ambiguous_countable

Classifying p-groups with one subgroup of order p

Cited
Amer. Math. Monthly 71, proof of Wedderburn’s theorem
Source
The note cites Hall’s classification: a p-group with exactly one subgroup of order p is cyclic or generalized quaternion. It also cites the solvability of groups whose Sylow subgroups are all cyclic.
What we did
We prove directly the special case the argument uses: if such a group is not cyclic, then p = 2 and it contains a, b of order 4 with bab−1 = a−1. For groups with cyclic Sylow subgroups we use Mathlib’s theory (IsZGroup). Mathlib’s own proof of Wedderburn’s theorem is switched off, and a dependency check confirms it is not used.

Blueprint lem:wb-pgroup, thm:wedderburn · Lean TJK.Wedderburn.mul_comm

Other quoted results, now proved

Cited
Several sources
Plane topology
The crosscut theorem replaces the dissertation’s “Figure 5” argument and the monotonicity of first hitting points. It is proved via continuous logarithms and homotopy lifting. Tietze’s theorem that path-connected Hausdorff spaces are arc-connected is also proved.
Analysis
We proved the facts about Poisson integrals (boundedness, boundary limits at continuity points, and the representation of harmonic functions whose Lp means are bounded, p > 1), together with Banach’s and the Lebesgue–Hausdorff–Banach theorems on Baire classes.
Set theory
We proved that an analytic non-Borel subset of ℝ exists (via a universal analytic set and diagonalization) and that a Vitali set exists.
Trans. AMS 141
Step (B)(III) cites a Bagemihl–McMillan theorem. We use the dissertation’s approximation theorem instead, as the paper itself suggests. Step (F)(I) cites a corollary of invariance of domain. We write down the inverse maps explicitly. The Hausdorff extension theorems for Baire-one functions, used in the dissertation and in Appendix (b), (c), are proved.
Sutcliffe
Sutcliffe’s Theorems 2 and 3, used in the note, are proved with explicit solutions.

Different arguments

In these places the source is correct, but the formal proof follows a different route.

Homeomorphisms of the disk

New proof
J. Math. Mech. 14, Theorem 1 (known only from its review)
Source
The review states the result: a boundary function of a self-homeomorphism of the disk is continuous off a countable set. No proof is available to us.
What we did
We give a new proof of a quantitative form: the points where φ has jumps of size at least ε number at most 2π/ε.

Shorter or more direct proofs

New proof
Dissertation and Trans. AMS 141
Theorem 9
The dissertation proves both inequalities for the measure of a union of disjoint segments at once, via Lemmas 23 and 24. We prove each inequality separately, more briefly.
Example after Theorem 9
For the null sets M0, M1 we take the Liouville numbers, a dense Gδ null set. Baire’s theorem is applied to an affine change of variable.
(XX)
For the existence of special functions we give a construction without recursion. The disjointness constraints turn out to be independent of the other member’s size.
Wedderburn, last step
The note takes a minimal normal subgroup of F*/Z and shows it is central. We use instead that the commutator subgroup of F* is cyclic, show by the same (1 + x) argument that it is central, and conclude that F* is nilpotent, hence cyclic.
Special families
Trans. AMS 141 also requires a special family to be nonempty. Nothing uses this, and we drop it.
